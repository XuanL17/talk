But Do These Agents Behave Like Real People?

Averages look right, conclusions don't [1]
Synthetic survey data resembles humans on average, but regressions often yield different inferences.
Flattened people [2]
LLMs misportray identity groups and give far less diverse responses than real participants.
Coin-flip replication [3]
Across 10 LLMs × 4 agent designs, the best agent matches human conclusions ~50% of the time (PAS ≤ 0.50).

Why Is There No Verifier for Human Behavior?

1
Scarce human data
Studies are slow, costly, IRB-bound, small-sample — and rarely shared in reusable form.
2
No single right answer
Behavior is a distribution across people and contexts; "correct" lives at the level of effects.
→ No verifier → no closed loop → "looks plausible".

Putting It Together: A Closed Loop for Human Behavior

Takeaways

1
Agents are still poor simulated humans
Best-case agreement with human conclusions ≈ coin flip; scale alone doesn't fix it.
2
Close the loop with explicit, quantitative specification
CoBRA: classic experiments as calibration tasks → measure, adjust, re-measure until on target — always relative to a named paradigm.
3
Next: the literature is the environment
Make published human studies executable, reviewable, and reusable (HumanStudy-Bench & Hub).
